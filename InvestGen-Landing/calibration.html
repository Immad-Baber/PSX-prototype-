Key Finding: Accuracy and Calibration Disagree on the Clear Trend Case

In the Clear Trend test case (SYS), HMM achieved an accuracy of 0.6489 and AUC-ROC of 0.7579. By these two metrics, HMM looks like a strong model. But its ECE was 0.3003, meaning its stated confidence was significantly off from actual outcomes.

Rule-Based had an accuracy of 0.6170, but its ECE was 0.0894. That means when it said 70% likely, the real hit rate was close to 70%. Its probability outputs were honest.

This is a concrete example of a model looking more accurate while being less honest about its own confidence. This is why the system uses Brier Score and ECE as primary evaluation metrics instead of accuracy alone. Accuracy tells you how often the model was right. ECE tells you whether the model knows when it does not know.

POC Finding & Quantitative Takeaway

Derived 100% from Real PSX Historical Data
Outcome: Trade-Off Discovered Between Stability & Probability Calibration

1. Whipsaw Resistance: In the High Volatility (OGDC) and Regime Transition (ENGRO) cases, the Hidden Markov Model (HMM) demonstrated superior state persistence with only a 3.3% flip rate (versus 40.0% for Rule-Based and 35.4% for GMM). Memoryless methods suffer severe whipsaw losses during sudden PSX pullbacks.

2. Probability Error (Brier Score): However, uncalibrated Gaussian emissions produce an average Brier error of 0.3709 (worse than the naive 0.25 baseline), while Rule-Based heuristics achieved 0.2417 on simple directional trends.

3. Architectural Conclusion: This strictly confirms our core architectural hypothesis: Raw HMM state transitions are necessary to prevent whipsaws, but cannot be used in isolation without the Genetic Algorithm calibration loop and Platt scaling to rectify raw emission probabilities.
